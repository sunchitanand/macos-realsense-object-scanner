Free-form RGB-D object capture

Move the camera around a fixed object and keep every frame easy to match

The D435i supplies metric depth, but it does not know its position in the room. The application estimates camera motion by matching each RGB-D frame to the previous accepted frame.

Best setup: one object, a clear flat support, textured surroundings, diffuse light, and a slow closed orbit.

Start with objects between roughly 10 and 50 centimetres. Shiny, transparent, black, thin, repetitive, or deforming objects are substantially harder.

Prepare the capture

REQUIRED

USB 3 connection

Connect the D435i directly with a SuperSpeed data cable. USB 2 will drop frames and destabilize registration.

REQUIRED

Object stays fixed

Move only the camera. If the object moves relative to the table or background, the scan cannot share one coordinate system.

HELPFUL

Textured mat

Place printed paper, painter’s tape, or another non-repeating texture around the object to improve camera tracking.

AVOID

Reflective geometry

Glass, mirrors, glossy black plastic, chrome, and hair-like details create missing or incorrect depth.

Capture a complete orbit

  1. 01
    Frame the whole objectKeep it centred and keep the background beyond your maximum depth setting where possible.
  2. 02
    Start at a distinctive viewA corner or asymmetrical feature is easier to recognize than a flat featureless face.
  3. 03
    Move slowly around the objectAim for only a few centimetres of camera movement between saved frames and at least 70% visual overlap.
  4. 04
    Add a higher and lower ringTilt down for the top, then lower the camera slightly for undersides visible above the support plane.
  5. 05
    Close the loopFinish near the exact starting view. A 30–60 second scan at 5 saved frames per second is a useful first attempt.

Understand the measurements

The application removes the dominant support plane, clusters the remaining geometry, and fits an oriented bounding box to the selected object cluster. Length, width, and height are the three principal-axis extents sorted from largest to smallest.

METRIC

Real-world scale

Depth values and the mesh are stored in metres. The interface reports the bounding-box extents in millimetres.

VERIFY

Not metrology-grade

Tracking drift, edge noise, occlusion, and support-plane extraction can change the result. Validate critical dimensions with calipers.

The exported object_printable.stl is rescaled from metres to millimetres so it imports at the expected size in common slicers. STL does not carry color. The application also reports whether the generated surface is watertight and manifold; a CHECK result should be repaired before printing.

Diagnose a bad scan

TRACKING

Duplicated or smeared surfaces

Move slower, increase texture, shorten the scan, and avoid sudden rotations or featureless close-ups.

DEPTH

Large holes

Change viewing angle and lighting; reduce glossy or transparent surfaces; keep the camera inside its useful range.

CLUSTER

Wrong object measured

Clear nearby clutter, reduce maximum depth, move the background farther away, and keep the intended object closest to the first view.

COVERAGE

Missing rear or top

Add overlapping height rings. Every surface must be visible in several depth frames to become a stable mesh.

Launch from Terminal

git clone https://github.com/sunchitanand/macos-realsense-object-scanner.git
cd macos-realsense-object-scanner
./run_object_scanner.sh

# Then open:
http://127.0.0.1:5055

The launcher installs the pinned macOS runtime and resets camera helpers automatically. A direct USB 3 data cable is strongly recommended.
